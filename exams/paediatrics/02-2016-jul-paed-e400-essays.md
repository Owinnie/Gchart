## Part II: Modified Essay Questions (MEQ) — 60 marks

### MEQ One

Juma, a 5 year old, is brought to the hospital because he refuses to walk. He complains of pain in the knee joints; sometimes he says the wrist and hands also hurt. He walks stiffly and with a slight limp. The attending clinician finds there is decreased range of motion and he has a low grade fever of 37.7°C. She considers Juvenile Rheumatoid Arthritis (JRA) high on the list of differential diagnosis.

a) List 5 other possible differential diagnoses for Juma's illness. **(5 marks)**

b) List the 3 subtypes of JRA based on clinical manifestation. **(3 marks)**

c) The clinician starts Juma on prednisolone right away, despite being aware that it has a number of adverse effects. Outline 10 side effects of steroid therapy. **(10 marks)**

d) Apart from pharmacotherapy, outline briefly 3 other aspects of management (i.e. non-drug management) that Juma and his family will benefit from. **(6 marks)**

e) List 6 complications that may arise from JRA. **(6 marks)**

### MEQ Two

Oscar, a 4 year old boy from Langa Langa, is brought to the local health centre where you are attached for your community experience. He looks like a 2 year old, is wasted, has an 'old man's appearance', has no interest in his surroundings and looks weak. His weight for height Z (WHZ) score is at -3.

a) List the 10 steps in the management of Oscar. **(5 marks)**

b) Apart from the nutritional condition that you think Oscar has, what other differentials will you consider in view of the WASTING he manifests? **(5 marks)**

c) Outline 10 complications that are possible from severe malnutrition. **(10 marks)**

d) Assuming Oscar has a weight of 12 kg, and looks dehydrated but not in shock, write down a sample treatment sheet to be used on the first day. Include your dosages. **(7 marks)**

e) List 3 relevant investigations that you will consider. **(3 marks)**
